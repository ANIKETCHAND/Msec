# MediShield — Scope, Limitations & Research Disclaimers

## 1. Prototype & Medical Disclaimer

**MediShield is an academic research prototype.**
- It is **not** a certified medical device under FDA, MDR, or ISO 13485 standards.
- It is **not** a clinical decision-support system (CDSS) and must **never** be used for clinical diagnoses, patient monitoring, treatment guidance, or clinical alarms.
- All device signals, telemetry metrics (heart rate, blood pressure, SPO2, infusion rates), patient identifiers, and network flow events are **100% synthetic**.
- No connections are made to real medical hardware, hospital networks, or protected health information (PHI).

---

## 2. Machine Learning & Detection Limitations

### 2.1 Distinction Between Predictions and Attacks
- ML classifications generated by the system are probabilistic outputs trained on network flow captures (CICIoMT2024).
- A high-confidence prediction indicates mathematical similarity to known training samples, **not definitive proof of an active malicious intrusion**.
- ML classifications must always be cross-referenced by human analysts with deterministic system logs and host evidence.

### 2.2 Dataset Representativeness & Leakage
- While UNB's CICIoMT2024 is a genuine and respected IoMT cybersecurity dataset, captured lab traffic does not capture all nuances of real-world hospital networks (e.g., proprietary wireless protocols, proprietary vendor telemetry, irregular packet jitter).
- Without group-aware cross-validation, models risk memorizing specific device MAC/IP artifacts rather than generalizable attack vectors. Preprocessing pipelines in MediShield explicitly address this.

---

## 3. Cryptographic & Security Boundaries

### 3.1 Key Management Limitations
- In this academic prototype, encryption keys (AES-GCM 256-bit) and application secrets are managed via server-side environment variables.
- In a production healthcare environment, key management must be delegated to dedicated Hardware Security Modules (HSMs) or enterprise KMS (e.g., AWS KMS, HashiCorp Vault) with hardware-backed tamper resistance and automated key rotation.

### 3.2 Integrity Verification Scope
- Storing SHA-256 cryptographic digests in a relational database verifies against payload corruption, transit modification, or unprivileged tampering.
- However, if an attacker achieves full superuser administrative access to the underlying PostgreSQL database, they could simultaneously alter both the synthetic payload and its stored hash. To guarantee non-repudiation in production, digests must be committed to immutable append-only ledgers or write-once-read-many (WORM) storage.
